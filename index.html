BEHIND THE NUMBERS

A sandbox, with visible rules.

Start at 19 in September 2026. Advance one month at a time for the first year, then one year at a time until age 25. Paychecks, bills and card spending still happen monthly during year mode.

The fictional card

$1,000 limit; 24% APR, or 29.99% while a penalty rate is active. New charges above the limit are declined; interest and fees can push a balance above it. If the grace period is inactive, the game applies APR ÷ 12 to the cycle balance. Real cards often calculate interest daily.

The minimum is the lesser of the balance and the greater of $25 or 1% of principal plus current interest and fees. A full payment restores the grace period immediately in this model; real agreements and trailing interest vary.

Paying below the minimum adds a fictional $30 fee, or $41 after another fee in six cycles, and advances lateness by 30 days. These amounts are game assumptions, not statements of legal limits. At 60 days the penalty rate starts; six on-time payments end it in this simplified model.

The simulated credit score

No score appears until six cycles. This educational 300–850 model weights payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, account age 15%, new credit 10%, and mix 10%. Its penalties and recovery speed are invented teaching mechanics—not the real FICO formula or a prediction of your actual score.
